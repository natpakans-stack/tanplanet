Design your own bouquet online

This isn't just a flower shop moved online. It's an experience designed to make sending flowers to someone special easy and meaningful.

Apr 13, 20266 min read

SHARE

Order a bouquet online, design it yourself, and send it straight to someone special.

FigmaUI/UX DesignE-commerceBuyer PortalSeller Portal

The problem

Why is ordering flowers online in Thailand still harder than it should be?

I wanted to send flowers for my partner's birthday, but I had to DM shops one by one and wait for each to reply. I could only pick from designs they already had, not the colors or flowers I wanted. In the end I didn't even know whether the flowers had arrived.

-- Insight from interviews with real users

Ordering means chatting with each shop

Most flower shops sell through social media. Customers DM one shop at a time and wait for a reply, a price and a confirmation. There is no page where they can just place an order.

No way to customize a bouquet

Customers can only pick from the shop's existing designs. Changing the color, adding flowers or choosing a size isn't possible; all they can do is ask for "something like this one."

Small shops have no platform to run on

Small florists log orders in Excel and count flower stock by hand. When orders pile up, things get mixed up or missed, and customers don't get their flowers on time.

No delivery tracking

After ordering, customers can't tell whether the flowers have arrived. To plan a surprise they have to call the shop, and the shop has to call the rider. Everyone loses time.

The solution

Two portals, designed so both buyers and sellers get an experience that works for them

Buyer Portal -- order and customize directly

A site where customers order a bouquet right away: choose the flowers, the color and the size, add a message card and pick a delivery date, all on one screen without DMing anyone.

Seller Portal -- the whole shop in one place

A dashboard for florists: see sales, accept orders, manage flower stock and read revenue reports in one place, with no more Excel.

Real-time Order Tracking

Customers and shops both see the order status in real time, from order accepted to bouquet arranged, out for delivery and delivered. Everyone can trust what they see.

Flower Customization UX

A customization flow that stays easy: progressive disclosure walks customers through one choice at a time, so the many options never feel overwhelming.

What I built

Full UI/UX for both portals, designed in Figma. Switch tabs to see the features.

Key Takeaways

What I learned designing the online flower shop

Design both sides at once, like two sides of a coin:

At first I focused only on the Buyer Portal and polished it a lot. Once I started on the Seller Portal, I found that some features that were convenient for buyers made the seller's job harder. "Pick any delivery time" sounds great to a customer, but a florist only has so much time to arrange bouquets. The lesson: think about both sides from the start, not as an afterthought.

"Flowers → color → size": three clicks to the right bouquet:

The first wireframe crammed every option onto one page: 15 flowers, 8 colors, 4 sizes, 3 wraps, cards, ribbons. Customers were confused and didn't know where to start. Switching to progressive disclosure, one step at a time with a live preview of the bouquet, made the user tests go smoothly. People liked that it felt like "making my own bouquet."

A good dashboard shows "what to do next," not just numbers:

The first Seller Dashboard had nice charts: monthly revenue, sales comparisons, a funnel chart. But florists wake up at 6 a.m. and have a short window before orders come in; they don't sit down to analyze data. Once the home screen said "3 orders waiting / 2 being arranged / 12 roses left," shops knew right away what to do first.

Flowers aren't parcels: "when it arrives" matters more than "has it arrived":

A parcel a day late is fine, but birthday flowers that arrive after midnight ruin the whole thing. Good order tracking says "arranging → on the way → arriving in 30 minutes" to both sender and recipient. For surprise orders especially, customers want to know the moment the flowers land so they can call right after.

One design system, two personalities:

The Buyer Portal should feel warm and pretty, like walking into a flower shop. The Seller Portal needs to be clean, functional and fast. Both use the same component library (buttons, cards, inputs, spacing) with different colors and layouts, so they read as one platform even though they look different. A good design system doesn't make every page identical; it makes them related.
