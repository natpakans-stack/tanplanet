Review: 44 days of WFH life behind a screen as a Product Design intern at LINE MAN Wongnai — what was it like? Let’s find out!

Feb 26, 202214 min read

SHARE
“Let me introduce myself first. My name is Tan, and I got the chance to join LINE MAN as a Product Design (UX/UI) intern during the WFH period.

Contents

  1. Wait! How did I even get into this internship?
  2. The interview + assignment experience
  3. What’s daily life like?
  4. Let me tell you about the work I got to do
  5. ‍What did I like here?
  6. Didn’t like (things to improve/fix)
  7. What went beyond my expectations?

And today I’d like to invite all of you readers to read a set of questions and answers that, before I knew it, I’d written both of myself (laughs)‍

Note : some of the images are taken from the Final Intern Presentation I made to present to the folks on the team so we could give each other feedback, in my last few days at work

Wait! How did I even get into this internship?

I was just about to graduate and was about to start going to job interviews. Because of this very poster (picture below), I thought I’d better do an internship first. At least in the next two months I’d find out for myself whether I actually enjoy doing this kind of work.

Cut to that moment: while I was scrolling through my feed, I came across their Facebook page posting that they were recruiting interns. On top of that, I’m a fan of Nai Hoi and the Spirit Medium. Once I saw this post, it didn’t take me long to decide. Thank you, Nai Hoi!

The interview + assignment experience

I think they email you an assignment to do and you have to send it back within 7 days (this probably depends on the position too). After that, you get an email letting you know whether you passed the assignment or not. (This one didn’t take long.)

Not long after, another email comes in to schedule an interview. I think there were 2 rounds (I don’t quite remember).

Personally, I felt the team focused on storytelling: what you’ve done before and what you got out of it, point one, two, three, four.

Basically, put in the effort and give it everything you can. As for the interview, just be natural. Don’t try to force it too much. Being yourself is best.

What’s daily life like?

Personally, I think the work schedule here is fairly clear, which makes things quite flexible for me and makes it much easier to manage my personal time.

There’s an HR team (oops, slap my mouth) that they call People here (Pee-Po!), constantly popping up with all kinds of recreational activities non-stop, whether it’s sing-along sessions by the staff, game tournaments, work intros or experience sharing.

They’re held on Friday evenings through various online channels.

And that’s not all! There are also discount codes for ordering food on LINE MAN for staff. Seriously great benefits... and that’s as an intern, so full-time staff probably get even more (haha)

Let me tell you about the work I got to do 

I was responsible for about four pieces of work. Some were short-term, week by week, like doing UX research for the Mart feature and designing stickers for the riders’ chat.

Or projects that took around 2-3 weeks, like designing the UI for the Rating and tipping features, plus notifications for the riders.

There was also a long-term, two-month project: revamping LINE MAN’s old official website. So mainly about 4 pieces.

‍

The first piece was UX research: analysing the pros and cons of both direct and indirect competitors, and directions for developing the feature in the short and long term. Finally, I pulled the findings together into a feedback presentation for the team to use going forward.

Of course, the main thing I got out of it was a ton of researching skill for finding the insights we need, and processes like these are just as essential for design.

The next piece was the stickers you’ll see when you chat with riders. Seeing riders actually use them, I was secretly thrilled they made it into the real thing, but it took quite a few versions to get there.

The goal of this project was to add more stickers to the chat so riders could talk to customers more easily.

The main thing I learned from this piece was adapting existing artwork into what we wanted, within various constraints. Reading the rules and regulations is also something you should understand before putting them into a design, and that turned out to be super useful.

Next was the project I probably spent the longest time on. The goal was to separate restaurant ratings and rider ratings more clearly, under a new design.

My part was the rider rating. The rating then leads into tipping the riders when you’re really satisfied.

In this case I learned a whole lot, from doing research that built on my earlier work, to having to dig into the details of the flow, including all the cases that can come up in different situations.

The last piece was improving the Website LINE MAN so users get a better experience, including bringing the content up to date. Of course, it also had to stay in line with the features of the LINE MAN app itself.

‍What did I like here?

The working atmosphere

It’s a great atmosphere to work in. There are sessions to sync and update each other on work, and critiques from seniors with different backgrounds, all aimed at making the work more well-rounded.

There are really fun retrospectives, where we help each other out with problems.

Colleagues (seniors)

It feels friendly. You can talk, you can ask, and you always get satisfying answers. It feels good to have people giving advice on the work I’m handling, listening to my ideas and adding new perspectives I might never have come across.

Didn’t like (things to improve/fix)
  • Work speed I feel like I still don’t work at as steady a pace as I should. It’ll take some time to adjust. These past 2 months felt like preparation for the real job.
  • Time management between work, rest and exercise doesn’t seem quite balanced yet. Being at home should make it easier, but it’s the opposite and I still haven’t got it right. These two months were a really good chance to adjust my work rhythm after graduating.
What went beyond my expectations?
  • Both teams working together : I have to say that, with the merger of 2 companies, LINE MAN and Wongnai, the part of the work I was in was split fairly clearly between them. There are some meetings each week where both teams come together to update each other, critique the work, comment and give each other feedback.
  • Adapting : It was much easier to adapt than I expected. No awkward gaps. It felt like being with friends, like being with seniors at my faculty, who kept serving up a friendly vibe and made me feel brave enough to (type out and) ask. Too bad we never got to meet face to face.
  • Got to explore a lot : I feel I got to do real, hands-on work, far more varied than I expected. It was full of things I didn’t know and lots of mistakes, and I tried hard not to repeat the same mistakes, because there really was so much to do.

‍
