Sometimes your dream faculty isn’t what you dreamed it would be

Jun 23, 202412 min read

SHARE
“While I was listening to a lecture at university.... if someone tossed out the question, which time of your life was the most fun?

Contents

  1. A time of fun
  2. A time of imagination
  3. A time of storms
  4. A time of decisions

A time of fun

That’s right, middle school. It’s probably the answer that rings true for a lot of people, an answer that doesn’t take much thinking at all.

I can picture myself with adrenaline pumping through my whole body. I can picture the school field at lunch break, a dozen or so kids playing football under the blazing sun.

No referee, no game plan, no rules whatsoever. All we needed was for our trusty sneakers to touch the grass, and to cheer together with our friends when the ball rolled past the water-bottle goalposts. That was it.

A life full of energy, as full as the sweat on our white school shirts, not caring what class was on in the afternoon, as long as we got to play first.

The beaming face of that boy said it all about how fun life was. A life with nothing much to think about: when it was time to study, you studied; when it was time to play, you went all in. There really was nothing more to it than that.

A time of imagination

After the time of fun... not long after, it was time to take the entrance exam for a new school. A new uniform was the sign that my life as a senior high school student had begun.

I still remember the moment I went to check the results with my mom, my hands shaking even though I was only fifteen, when I saw my own name on the board: “Students admitted to Grade 10.”

It didn’t take me long to settle in with new friends. My high school life was surrounded by friends who’d drag each other to tutoring after school, mostly preparing for the subjects they needed for the entrance exams to their dream faculties.

Our greetings when we ran into each other turned into the question: which faculty do you want to study?

I tried to imagine myself in all kinds of careers, but my answer was never clear. All I knew was that I had to study really hard and collect as many good grades as I could, so I could pick any faculty once I figured out what I wanted to study.

Phew! If you’ve read this far, you probably know it wasn’t easy at all. In some classes I just couldn’t keep up, and I couldn’t do the homework, but I tried to stick with my friends and gritted my teeth until I scraped through, battered and bruised.

In the end, the effort paid off. My GPA was good enough to apply through the quota for several interesting faculties, and one of them was the Faculty of ICT at Mahidol. From that day on, I pinned it as my dream faculty, without even knowing what they actually studied there.

Life back then felt a lot more relaxed. I started wanting to join all sorts of school activities, to make up for the high school years I’d missed out on.

So I decided to run for the student committee, joined the cheering and card-stunt teams, sat on the student council, and did much more than you can probably imagine, until my life in the classroom was completely replaced by activities.

All with the thought that I’d still have plenty of time to start again from square one once I got to university.

A time of storms

“What’s your name? What’s your name?” The song from the MC’s microphone looped over and over, in time with the drums from the seniors of the faculty, who stood there welcoming us and trying, in a friendly way, to get to know all the first-year freshies.

As usual, for me, making friends at university wasn’t as hard as many people worry it will be. What turned out to be hard were the courses in the faculty.

That’s right, you guessed right again. My plan to start from square one at university was a huge letdown. Maybe because I hadn’t looked into the curriculum beforehand at all. Before I knew it, I was probably starting from negative numbers.

I wanted to learn Photoshop and model sculpting, but what I actually got to study was nowhere near that. My feelings started raising their hands in protest, pointing out that this was not at all what I’d dreamed of.

All I could do was hypnotise myself into believing I had to work harder than before and study even more seriously. And with help from my friends and my lovely senior buddy, I stumbled my way through freshman life.

Stepping into my second year in the faculty, I started not really understanding what was taught in class, and on top of that I wasn’t paying much attention. I’d lost my passion for whatever was on the board.

When it came time for solo projects, it was every man for himself. My friends could barely keep up themselves and had no time to help anyone else. I chose to do as much as I could.

My determination started to fade, while the material in class got exponentially harder with every lesson. My confusion kept growing bigger and bigger.

Until the day came when I had to pick up a pen and put my confusion down on the answer sheet in the exam room. A pile of exam papers lay on the desk, in stark contrast to the confidence I barely had left.

I guessed by gut feeling and, as expected, gut feelings really aren’t meant for exam rooms. My scores didn’t come out well, and at worst I even got an F in some subjects.

My academic advisor started calling me in for talks more and more often. My cumulative GPA was worryingly close to academic probation.

I started wanting to drop out and re-sit the entrance exams for another faculty that suited me better. Luckily, my dad and mom gave me the freedom to decide, and always encouraged me and had my back.

Before deciding on a new path, I got the chance to talk with my senior buddy. In our whole buddy line there were only the two of us. We were super close, and super lonely too (haha).

Her name was P’Mooyor. I spent hours pouring my feelings out to my audience of one.

Her face stayed perfectly calm as she firmly said no. She told me I could make it in this faculty, that there were still lots of things waiting for me here, and that she believed I could do them well.

I looked puzzled. She put on her serious face once more and said,

"Tan, you’ve probably heard that we should do what we love, that what we do should be full of passion, and then we’ll do it well. Those words are only half true. In real life, we have to get our hands dirty. We have to learn things we don’t like, many times over, and we have to get through them."

All I could do was sit there blinking, nodding along as the picture became crystal clear. She wrapped up by adding that she had dropped out and started over too, which was probably why she understood my situation so perfectly.

A time of decisions

I chose to believe her, and to believe in myself once again. I shifted into forward gear like a kid who was serious about studying, putting real effort into every subject I liked, while not forgetting to open my mind to the subjects I didn’t like along the way.

Before long, I made it to choosing my major in third year. I found things I liked and was good at, just as much as things I didn’t like, and it seemed there were even more of those than of the things I liked.

But that’s how it goes, dear readers. When a boat has a good rudder, it will carry us all the way to the destination we hoped for.

My boat is no different. It has sailed for four years now, and it may be time to change boats, change course and set a new destination on the map.

I hope this boat sails through life’s new kinds of storms out at sea, just like it always has.

This expensive lesson doesn’t just tell the story of how little attention I paid to the curriculum I chose for my university life. It also shows another side of the truth about life.

Sometimes the things in our dreams are full of beauty, but when we finally see them right in front of us, real life may be surrounded by thorns. Many things may not be what we dreamed of, but in the end we’ll have to get through them someday.

Good night.
