Empathize · Define

The problem isn't that AI makes ugly images. It's that nobody writes it a script.

Every platform's feed is full of content stamped out with the same tools, and people have learned to spot it and scroll past in the first second

The clip that sparked this idea didn't win because its visuals were better than anyone else's. It had one joke, one twist, and ended right away. Hundreds of thousands of views. What the market lacks isn't production capacity; it's the craft of writing.

— An observation that became the starting hypothesis

Volume without a script

Many channels use AI to multiply clips, not to tell stories. The result is content that follows every rule of the formula except the one that makes people watch to the end.

Shipping the tool's default look

No channel style of their own, just the default output of a model everyone uses. Viewers can't recognize the channel from its visuals and know immediately that it's generated.

Clips die within two days

Trend-chasing content spikes fast and then it's over. You have to keep hunting for new topics, and it turns into a full-time job with no days off instead of side income.

Thai narration is where generic tools break

A tonal language makes generic synthetic voices get the rhythm and words wrong, especially technical terms and numbers, which happen to be the heart of explainer clips.

Define

Redefining the advantage: not personality, but pipeline and taste

The real goal is side income, without being on camera and without becoming the face of the channel. So the advantage has to come from somewhere else.

Can build a pipeline

Script in → clip out as an automated line. Cost per clip is lower and more consistent than generating each clip by hand. Software people can do this; most creators can't.

Systematic art direction

I wrote the channel's visual rules like design tokens. Every clip shares one look, so people recognize the channel from its visuals. That's the line between work that looks intentional and work that looks mass-produced.

Product thinking

Set a repeatable format, measure retention and iterate like UX work, instead of judging success by the views of a single clip

Ideate

What's locked, and what's deliberately left open

Only decisions that affect cost and the channel's look are locked. The rest waits for the pilot clip to answer.

TopicStatusReasoning
Aspect ratioLockedVertical, because it matches how people watch and the platforms I'm targeting. It can still be re-rendered as horizontal from the same assets later.
LengthLockedEvery clip is the same length as a channel rule. That makes retention genuinely comparable and forces the script to be sharp.
Topic criteriaLockedOnly knowledge that will still hold up five years from now. The channel becomes a library that accumulates views from search, not a feed that has to chase trends.
Visual rulesLockedEverything on screen must look cut out and pasted: people, animals, objects and maps. Nothing photorealistic floating outside this visual language.
NarrationNot lockedI plan to compare several real providers using a script with hard words and numbers before deciding. There's also a zero-cost option, narrating it myself, which may be best for the channel's identity too.
Channel nameNot lockedDeliberately not naming it until the pipeline is proven, because a name chosen before you know what the channel is usually has to change later.
The finding that changed the cost of the whole project

This format doesn't need full-clip video generation. Almost all of it is art-directed still images plus motion done in code, which is many times cheaper than generating video and gives full control over style. Real video is reserved for the opening shot or shots that truly need to impress.

Prototype · Test

Cost estimated from real tests, not from pricing pages

Real generation tests showed that auto mode picked an expensive model on its own, which made my first estimate off from reality by several times

Auto mode picked an expensive model, so cost per clip shot up unnoticed

In the first test I let the tool choose the model. The images were good, but the price per image was several times higher than it should have been. If I had calculated cost per clip from that round, I would have wrongly concluded the business model doesn't work.

Fix: for real production, lock a budget model in place and recalculate cost from that price. Clips made entirely of stills then fit a monthly budget in the hundreds of baht, enough for about two clips a week comfortably — Lesson: unit cost has to be measured with the settings you'll actually use, not the defaults.

The thing I wanted to do first is the thing that should come last

The urge at the start was to name the channel, make a logo and launch. Fun work, but it doesn't answer whether the formula can actually be repeated.

Fix: lock the order: finish one pilot clip first to measure the real time and credits → make two more with the same template to see if the effort really drops → launch the channel only when three clips are ready to publish — a new channel with content looks more credible than an empty one with a nice logo.

Fast views or lasting views: it seemed like I had to choose

Trending topics bring traffic fast but die fast. Evergreen topics last but grow slowly. Picking one side means giving up the other entirely.

Fix: use trending news as the hook, but make the content evergreen knowledge. It gets traffic from the trend now and keeps being found in search for years. It also avoids locking the channel into a narrow topic.
Prototype

A production line designed so effort per clip keeps dropping

The heavy work moves into the template, which is written once. Each following clip is just filling the template.

01

Script and research

AI helps find and draft, but a person checks every fact, because one mistake in an explainer clip costs the credibility of the whole channel

02

Storyboard as stills

Break the script into a set number of images per clip, a unit where cost is controllable, unlike thinking in seconds of video

03

Generate images by the channel's visual rules

Every image must use the same cut-out language. An off-tone image gets regenerated or cut, never kept just to save credits.

04

Narration

Compare several real providers with a script full of hard words, with a good fallback: narrating it myself, which costs nothing and sounds most natural

05

Assemble with a template written in code

Every clip is the script and images dropped into the same template, then rendered. No manual editing. This is a real advantage for someone with a software background.

Define

Success criteria set in advance, before any numbers exist

Set now, because setting them after seeing results turns into rationalizing whatever numbers came in

  • Completion rate matters more than follower count — the question is whether the joke or the content reaches the end, not how many people hit follow
  • At least one clip where people talk about the content, not about it being made with AI — if every comment is about the tools, the craft hasn't passed yet
  • Measure whether the formula really repeats — time each clip from script to finished render, and check whether the second and third clips really take less time
  • The first income comes from affiliate links, not ads — I set the expectation plainly that the first three months will be close to zero, so I don't quit halfway because of wrong expectations

Define

What's deliberately out of scope

This project wrote down what it “won't do” before starting, because this kind of work drifts off course very easily

  • No mass-producing low-quality clips every day — volume is what the market already has too much of. Competing there is a game lost from the start.
  • No clickbait or shock content — it gets views fast but destroys the reason people would come back to the channel
  • No posting to every platform from day one — start with one channel to prove the formula, then repost the same files later at almost no extra cost
  • No topics that expire — news, trends and match results are all cut, because they conflict with the goal of making something once and collecting results for a long time
