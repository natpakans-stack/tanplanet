Explainer videos don't lose on content. They lose on the first frame

However good the content is, if the picture doesn't move, viewers leave before the interesting part.

Stills and text get scrolled past

A still image with narration over it looks like a slide. Viewers leave before the story gets going.

Key numbers pass too fast to remember

A comparison like "500,000 baht a month versus 16 billion baht", if only spoken, never lets viewers see how big the gap really is.

AI images that look like AI

Undirected AI images all look alike. Viewers spot them right away and stop trusting the content too.

Designing per episode means no consistent style

Redesigning every episode leaves the channel without its own look, and each video takes too long to keep up a regular schedule.

How it works — 5 steps from script to a clip ready to post

The parts that need a human eye, I do myself. The repetitive parts go to the machine.

01

Script and narration

Record the Thai voiceover line by line, then transcribe it back to check every word was read correctly before laying in visuals.

02

Direct the visuals as one set

Lock the visual style for the whole story first, then generate each scene to match, color-grade them together, and reject anything that looks obviously AI.

03

Compose in Remotion Studio

Time the cards, text and camera moves to the audio, with a live preview and no render needed.

04

Render on an always-on machine

Renders are sent to a home server. A clip of about 1 minute takes around 8 minutes, and I can keep working in the meantime.

05

Check before release

Check that text stays inside the safe area, no frames flicker, and every line has the same loudness before it goes into the posting queue.

Test & Iterate

Four lessons from cutting real videos

Most came from watching finished clips and feeling "not quite there yet".

The prettiest shot isn't always the best shot

The first showreel used shots with nice images and clear text, but cut together they looked like slides. Nothing said this was motion work.

Fix: pick moments where something happens, like a document being pulled up and highlighted, a number counting up, or the camera diving, and cut so each clip reaches the moment that pays the viewer off.

The camera moves most at the start of a scene

Most painted scenes move the camera hardest early on, then slow down. Cutting from the middle gets you only the near-still part.

Fix: cut in from the first second of the scene to get the full motion, and cut out before the image settles.

Each scene looked good, but together they weren't one story

Generating scenes one at a time with no shared rules produced images with such different colors and linework that it felt like watching different stories.

Fix: lock the style before the first image (palette, linework and texture) and apply the same rules to every scene. All 13 scenes of The Silver Jar Underground now read as one set of paintings.

The Thai voiceover mispronounced words and varied in loudness

The synthetic voice misread proper names and some Thai words, and each line came out at a different volume, which made it jarring to listen to.

Fix: keep a reusable pronunciation dictionary, transcribe every line back and compare it to the script, and normalize every line to the same loudness.
